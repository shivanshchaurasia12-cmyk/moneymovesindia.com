Overtrading: Why More Trades Isn't a Better Strategy

The quiet mistake of trading too often, and why it usually has more to do with boredom than opportunity.

6 min read Money Moves India Updated 2026

Overtrading means placing trades more frequently than your strategy actually calls for — often out of boredom, impatience, or a feeling that you should always be "doing something" in the market.

Why it's harder to notice than it sounds

Overtrading rarely feels reckless in the moment — each individual trade might look reasonable on its own. The damage tends to show up gradually, through accumulated transaction costs and a string of lower-conviction trades that wouldn't have met your criteria on a calmer day.

The cost that's easy to underestimate

Every trade carries a cost, whether that's the spread, a commission, or both. A strategy that would be profitable at ten well-chosen trades a month can quietly become unprofitable at fifty lower-quality ones, purely from those costs adding up.

A simple check worth using

Before entering a trade, it's worth asking honestly whether it actually meets your usual criteria, or whether you're placing it mainly because you feel like you should be trading right now. If it's the latter, that's usually a sign to wait.

Worth knowingSome traders set a maximum number of trades per day or week as a hard rule, specifically to remove the temptation to trade out of restlessness rather than genuine opportunity.
