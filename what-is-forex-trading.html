What Is Forex Trading? A Plain-Language Start

The absolute basics of currency trading, explained the way your first tutorial should have gone.

7 min read Money Moves India Last updated: 18 Aug 2026

Forex trading means buying one currency while selling another, hoping the exchange rate moves in your favour. "Forex" is just short for "foreign exchange" — the same market that decides how many rupees you get when you exchange money for a trip abroad, except here, traders are trying to profit from those rate changes instead of just spending the money.

How a currency pair actually works

Every forex trade involves two currencies, written as a pair — for example, USD/INR. The first currency (USD) is the "base," and the second (INR) is the "quote." If USD/INR is trading at 86.20, that means 1 US dollar costs ₹86.20. If you believe the dollar will get stronger against the rupee, you "buy" the pair. If you think the opposite, you "sell" it.

Who's actually on the other side of the trade?

It's easy to imagine forex as one person trading against another randomly, but the real market is made up of banks, governments, large companies moving money across borders, and retail traders like an individual using a trading app. Retail traders access this market through a broker, who connects your order to the wider market.

Why prices move at all

Exchange rates shift based on things like interest rate decisions by central banks (RBI, US Federal Reserve), inflation data, political events, and overall demand for a currency. None of this is random — it's reactive to real economic news, which is why serious traders follow financial news rather than guessing.

Quick reality checkForex is open nearly 24 hours a day across different global sessions, unlike the Indian stock market which has fixed trading hours. That constant activity is part of why it attracts so many beginners — and also why it can be risky for someone learning without a plan.

What beginners usually get wrong

Most people don't lose money because they misunderstood what a currency pair is. They lose money because they started trading with real money before understanding position size, leverage, and risk management — the practical mechanics covered in the next few guides on this site. Understanding what forex is is step one. Knowing how much of your money to actually put on a single trade is a completely separate skill.

A reasonable way to start

  • Learn the vocabulary first — pip, lot, leverage, spread (see our next guide)
  • Practise on a demo/paper trading account before using real money
  • Only use money you can genuinely afford to lose, and treat this as learning, not income, for a long time
  • Check whether the platform you're considering is legal for Indian residents to use — this varies more than most tutorials mention
