What Is Dropshipping? A Realistic Look for Beginners

How the model actually works, what it costs to start, and where most beginners lose money.

8 min read Money Moves India Updated 2026

Dropshipping is a way of selling products online without holding any inventory yourself. When a customer buys from your online store, you forward the order to a supplier, who ships the product directly to the customer. You never touch the product — you're essentially running the marketing and customer-facing side of a store, while someone else handles manufacturing, storage, and shipping.

How the money actually flows

You set your own selling price, which is higher than what the supplier charges you. The difference is your profit, minus advertising costs, platform fees, and payment processing fees. For example, if a supplier sells an item to you for ₹300 and you sell it for ₹700, your gross margin is ₹400 — before subtracting what you spent on ads to get that sale in the first place.

Why it's popular with beginners

  • Low upfront cost compared to holding physical inventory
  • No warehouse, packing, or shipping logistics to manage yourself
  • Can technically be started from a laptop and a phone

Where beginners actually lose money

The reality most "dropshipping guru" videos leave out is that advertising costs, not product sourcing, are usually what determines whether you make money. Running ads on platforms like Meta or Google costs real money whether or not anyone buys, and beginners frequently spend more on ads chasing sales than they earn back. Slow supplier shipping times (sometimes weeks, if sourcing internationally) also lead to refund requests and unhappy customers, which eat further into margin.

Worth knowingA profitable dropshipping business usually only becomes clear after testing several products and ad approaches — most individual product tests lose money before one works. Budgeting for that testing phase, rather than expecting immediate profit, is a more realistic way to start.

A more realistic starting approach

  1. Start with a very small, controlled ad budget you're fully prepared to lose while testing what works.
  2. Pick a niche you actually understand — it's much easier to write convincing product descriptions and ads for something you'd genuinely use.
  3. Prioritise suppliers with faster, more reliable shipping, even if their per-unit cost is slightly higher — customer trust matters more long-term than a few extra rupees of margin.
  4. Track your numbers properly — ad spend, product cost, platform fees, and refunds, so you know your real profit, not just your revenue.

Is it a good fit for students?

Dropshipping can be started around school or college hours since there's no physical stock to manage, which is part of its appeal for students. But it's not passive income — successful stores usually involve real time spent on ad testing, customer service, and store management, especially in the early months.
