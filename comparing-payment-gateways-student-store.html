Comparing Payment Gateways for a Small Student Store

How to actually get paid when you're selling online, and what each option costs you.

7 min read Money Moves India Updated 2026

If you're running a small store — on Shopify, Instagram, or your own site — how you collect payment matters almost as much as what you sell. Here's what actually differs between the common options available to a small or student-run store in India.

UPI-first gateways

Gateways built around UPI let a customer pay by scanning a QR code or entering a UPI ID, with money landing in your account almost instantly. For small transactions — which is most of what a student store sells — this tends to have the lowest friction: no card details to type, no redirect to a different page, and no app download required on the customer's end. The trade-off is that UPI-only setups can be limiting if you ever sell to customers outside India.

Full payment gateway platforms

Larger gateways bundle UPI, cards, net banking, and wallets into a single checkout, and usually offer a proper dashboard to track transactions, refunds, and settlements. They're more suited to a store that's grown past a handful of orders a week, since the setup involves more verification steps — business documents, bank details, sometimes a review period — before you can start accepting payments.

Buy-now-pay-later and installment options

Some gateways let customers split a payment into installments, which can raise conversion on higher-priced items but adds complexity to your refund and accounting process, since the money doesn't arrive as one clean transaction. For a small store selling low-cost items, this option usually isn't worth the added complexity.

Fees, settlement time, and what to actually compare

The number that matters most is the transaction fee percentage, since it eats directly into your margin on every single sale — even a 1% difference adds up fast at volume. The second number that matters is settlement time: how many days after a sale the money actually reaches your bank account, since a slow settlement cycle can strain cash flow if you're using sales revenue to buy your next batch of stock.

What to pick as a student starting out

For a first store with low order volume, a UPI-first gateway with simple setup and same-day or next-day settlement is usually the most practical choice. You can always add a fuller gateway later once order volume justifies the extra setup and verification work.

Documents and verification you'll likely need

Most gateways ask for some proof that a real person or business is behind the account before releasing funds properly — typically a PAN card, a bank account in your name or a family member's, and sometimes a business registration depending on the gateway and your order volume. If you're a minor running a small store, it's common and reasonable to set the account up under a parent's or guardian's name and PAN until you're old enough to register one yourself. Trying to fake or skip this step usually just gets an account frozen mid-sale, which is a far bigger problem than the short delay of doing it properly the first time.

What happens when a payment fails or a customer disputes a charge

Every gateway will show you a small percentage of failed or reversed payments — a card declined, a UPI request timed out, a customer disputing a charge after the fact. What separates a good gateway from a frustrating one is how clearly it shows you why a payment failed and how fast disputes get resolved. Before committing to one, search for recent reviews mentioning refund and dispute handling specifically, not just the sign-up process, since that's the part you'll actually notice once you're running real orders.

Worth knowingAlways test the full checkout flow yourself with a small real payment before launching — a broken or confusing checkout page is one of the most common reasons small stores lose sales they'd otherwise have made.
