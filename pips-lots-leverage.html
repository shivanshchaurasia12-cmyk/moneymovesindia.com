Pips, Lots & Leverage — Explained Without the Jargon

The three words every trading app throws at you on day one, broken down with real numbers.

6 min read Money Moves India Updated 2026

If you've opened a trading app and immediately hit words like "pip," "lot," and "leverage" without any explanation, you're not alone in finding it confusing. These three ideas control how much money you actually make or lose on a trade, so it's worth slowing down here.

Pip: the smallest standard price move

A pip is usually the fourth decimal place in a currency pair's price. If USD/INR moves from 86.2050 to 86.2150, that's a 100 pip move. Pips are just a standard unit for measuring how much a price changed, so traders across the world can compare movement using the same language.

Lot: how much you're actually trading

A lot is a fixed trade size. A standard lot is typically 100,000 units of the base currency, but brokers also offer mini lots (10,000 units) and micro lots (1,000 units) — which matter a lot for beginners, because trading a full standard lot with a small account is how accounts get wiped out fast. The lot size you choose directly decides how much each pip movement is worth in real money.

Leverage: borrowed exposure, not borrowed money in your pocket

Leverage lets you control a larger position than your actual account balance. For example, 1:100 leverage means ₹10,000 in your account can control a ₹10,00,000 position. This sounds powerful, and it is — in both directions. Leverage doesn't just multiply your potential profit, it multiplies your potential loss by the same amount.

Worth knowingHigh leverage is the single biggest reason beginner trading accounts blow up quickly. A 1% adverse price move against a highly leveraged position can wipe out a large chunk of your account balance in minutes.

Putting it together with a simple example

Say you trade 1 micro lot (1,000 units) of USD/INR, and the price moves 50 pips in your favour. Depending on the pip value for that lot size, that might be a gain of a few hundred rupees — modest, and manageable to understand. Now imagine trading 10 standard lots with high leverage on a small account: the same 50 pip move could represent a life-changing amount of money, in either direction. That gap is exactly why position sizing matters more than most beginners realise.

A practical rule some traders use

Many risk-conscious traders never risk more than 1–2% of their account balance on a single trade. It's not a legal rule, just a common discipline — and it's a useful anchor while you're still learning how leverage and lot size interact.
