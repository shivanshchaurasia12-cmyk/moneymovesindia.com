Print-on-Demand Explained: T-shirts Without Inventory

How to sell custom-designed products without ever holding stock — and what it actually costs to try.

7 min read Money Moves India Updated 2026

Print-on-demand lets you sell custom-designed products — t-shirts, mugs, phone cases — without holding any physical inventory. A supplier only prints and ships an item after a customer actually buys it.

How the process works

You create a design, upload it to a print-on-demand platform connected to your online store, and set your selling price. When someone orders, the platform's manufacturing partner prints and ships the item directly to the customer. You keep the difference between your price and the platform's base cost.

What it actually costs to start

The appeal is a low upfront cost compared to traditional retail — no bulk inventory to buy. That said, there are still real costs: your store platform's subscription or transaction fees, and typically some spend on marketing to actually get customers to your product.

Where the margins actually sit

Per-item margins on print-on-demand tend to be slimmer than platforms make it look, once shipping and the base manufacturing cost are factored in — profitability usually depends on selling in reasonable volume, not on any single high-margin sale.

Worth knowingShipping times and costs for print-on-demand products can be longer and higher than customers expect from established online retailers, which is a common source of customer complaints for new sellers.

What actually differentiates sellers

Since anyone can use the same base products and printing suppliers, design quality and a specific niche audience — rather than the product category itself — are usually what separate print-on-demand stores that gain traction from ones that don't.
