How to Register a Business in India as a Minor

What the law actually says about under-18 entrepreneurs, and the workaround most student sellers use.

7 min read Money Moves India Updated 2026

A lot of student side-hustle content skips this entirely, but it matters: under Indian contract law, a person under 18 generally cannot enter into a legally binding contract on their own — and running a formal business involves contracts constantly, from supplier agreements to platform terms of service to bank accounts.

What this means practically

It means a minor typically cannot independently open a current (business) bank account, register for GST as a sole proprietor in their own name, or sign formal seller agreements with marketplaces. This isn't a rule specific to any one platform — it comes from the Indian Contract Act's treatment of minors' agreements as generally void.

The common, legitimate workaround

Most under-18 students who want to run a real, formally registered business do it with a parent or guardian as the legal owner on paper — the business bank account, GST registration, and marketplace seller account are opened in the parent's name, with the parent's informed involvement and consent. The student can still do all the actual day-to-day work (sourcing, marketing, customer service, order management), but the legal and financial responsibility sits with the adult.

Worth knowingThis isn't about hiding anything — being upfront with a parent about wanting to run a small business and asking them to be the registered owner is a completely normal, common path for young entrepreneurs in India, and it protects both of you legally.

What you can often do without a parent's name

  • Informal resale through social media (sharing product links, taking orders manually) without a formal registered business — though income above certain thresholds may still have tax implications your family should be aware of
  • Learning platforms, building skills, and even freelancing for clients who are comfortable working informally with a minor, though formal contracts still generally need a guardian involved

When formal registration actually becomes necessary

Once you're selling at real volume — particularly on marketplaces like Flipkart or Amazon that require GST registration for most sellers — you'll need the proper paperwork, meaning you'll need a parent or guardian involved as the legal registrant if you're still under 18. It's worth having that conversation early, before you've built up a business you then have to restructure.

Turning 18

Once you turn 18, you can register a business, open a current account, and sign contracts independently in your own name — at which point everything set up under a parent's name can potentially be transferred or re-registered under yours, depending on the platform and business structure.
