Revenge Trading: The Mistake That Wipes Out Accounts

The emotional spiral that turns one manageable loss into a much bigger one.

6 min read Money Moves India Updated 2026

Revenge trading is what happens when a trader, frustrated by a loss, immediately jumps back into the market trying to "win it back" — often with a bigger position size and far less discipline than the trade that just failed.

Why it happens

A loss doesn't just cost money — it triggers a genuine emotional response, and the impulse to immediately fix it is a natural human reaction. The problem is that trading decisions made from that emotional state tend to abandon whatever strategy or risk rules were being followed a moment earlier.

What it usually looks like

Common signs include increasing position size right after a loss, entering a trade without the usual analysis, or skipping a stop-loss because "this one has to work." Individually these might seem like small deviations — together, they compound risk quickly.

A practical way to interrupt the pattern

Many experienced traders build in a mandatory pause after a loss — stepping away from the screen for a set period, or capping the number of trades allowed in a single day — specifically to remove the option of trading on impulse.

Worth knowingIf you notice you're increasing your position size specifically because you just lost money, that's usually a clearer signal to stop for the day than to keep going.
