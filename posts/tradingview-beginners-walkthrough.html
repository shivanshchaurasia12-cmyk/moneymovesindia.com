TradingView for Beginners: A Walkthrough

One of the most widely used charting tools — and how to make sense of it on your first visit.

7 min read Money Moves India Updated 2026

TradingView is one of the most widely used charting platforms among retail traders, largely because a solid free version is available before you ever need to pay for anything. Here's how to get oriented on your first visit.

The basic chart layout

When you search for a symbol — a currency pair, stock, or index — you'll land on a price chart. The toolbar on the left lets you draw trend lines and shapes; the top bar controls the timeframe (from one-minute to monthly candles) and chart type.

Indicators

The "Indicators" button opens a large library of technical tools — moving averages, RSI, MACD, and many more — that can be added on top of the price chart. As a beginner, it's easy to add too many at once; starting with one or two well-understood indicators tends to be more useful than a cluttered chart.

Watchlists and alerts

You can build a watchlist of assets you're following, and set price alerts that notify you when a symbol hits a specific level — useful for keeping an eye on multiple markets without constantly checking the app.

Worth knowingTradingView is a charting and analysis tool, not a broker — you can't place real trades directly through the free version. It's meant to be paired with your actual trading platform, not to replace it.

What the free plan covers

The free tier is generous enough for most beginners: real-time or slightly delayed data depending on the market, a solid indicator library, and multiple saved charts. Paid tiers mainly add more simultaneous indicators, alerts, and faster data — worth considering later, not on day one.
