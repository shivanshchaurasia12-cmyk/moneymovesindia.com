5 Common Mistakes New Traders Make

The patterns that quietly wipe out beginner accounts — and how to notice them in yourself before they do.

7 min read Money Moves India Last updated: 18 Aug 2026

Almost nobody blows up a trading account because of one bad decision. It's usually a handful of small, repeatable habits — the same five mistakes, in slightly different outfits, over and over. If you can spot them in yourself early, you skip most of the expensive lessons other beginners pay for the hard way.

1. Trading without any written plan

Opening a trading app and "seeing what looks good" is not a strategy — it's guessing with money attached. A plan doesn't need to be complicated. It just needs to say, before you enter a trade, what would prove you right, what would prove you wrong, and how much you're willing to lose if you're wrong. Without that written down somewhere, every decision gets made emotionally, in the moment, which is exactly when your judgement is weakest.

2. Risking too much on a single trade

New traders often size a position based on how confident they feel, not on how much they can actually afford to lose. A common rule among more experienced traders is to risk only 1-2% of total capital on any single trade. That way, even a string of losses doesn't wipe out the account — it just stings. Betting big on a "sure thing" is how one bad trade turns into a closed account.

3. Revenge trading after a loss

Taking a loss and immediately jumping back in to "win it back" is one of the fastest ways to turn a small loss into a large one. The trade after a loss is rarely made with a clear head — it's made with frustration, and frustration doesn't read charts well. The better move after a loss is almost always to step away, not to double down.

4. Ignoring or moving the stop-loss

A stop-loss exists to cap your damage automatically, before emotion has a chance to talk you out of it. New traders frequently either skip setting one, or move it further away mid-trade, hoping the price will "come back." Sometimes it does. Often it doesn't, and a small manageable loss becomes a much bigger one because the safety net was removed right when it was needed most.

5. Copying signals or tips blindly

Following a stranger's "buy now" call in a Telegram group without understanding why they're making that call means you have no idea when to exit, and no way to tell a good call from a lucky guess. If a trade goes wrong, you're left holding a position you never really understood in the first place.

Quick reality checkEvery one of these mistakes is avoidable, and none of them require more market knowledge to fix — they require more discipline around the trades you already know how to make.

How to actually fix this

  • Write your entry and exit rules down before you place a trade, not while you're in it
  • Decide your maximum risk per trade in advance, as a fixed percentage of your capital
  • Build a rule that after two losses in a row, you stop trading for the day
  • Always set a stop-loss the moment you enter a trade — and don't move it further away
  • Practise on a demo or paper trading account until these habits feel automatic, not effortful
