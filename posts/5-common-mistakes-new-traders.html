5 Common Mistakes New Traders Make

The patterns that quietly wipe out beginner accounts — and how to notice them in yourself before they do.

7 min read Money Moves India Updated 2026

Almost nobody starts trading and gets it right immediately. But some mistakes are so common that recognising them in advance can save you a lot of money and frustration.

1. Trading with money you can't afford to lose

This is the single biggest one. If a loss would affect your rent, your fees, or your family's finances, that money shouldn't be in a trading account. Trading with pressure on your shoulders leads to worse decisions, not better ones.

2. Skipping a demo account entirely

It's tempting to jump straight to real money because demo trading "doesn't feel real." But a demo account is exactly where you should make your first mistakes — mixing up an order type, misjudging leverage, panicking during a fast price move — because none of it costs you anything there.

3. No stop-loss, or moving it after the fact

A stop-loss is an order that automatically closes your position once it hits a certain loss level, protecting you from a small loss turning into a huge one. New traders often either skip setting one, or move it further away mid-trade, hoping the price will "come back." That single habit is behind a huge share of beginner account blowups.

4. Risking too much on one trade

Putting a large chunk of your account into a single trade means one bad move can undo weeks of careful gains. Experienced traders typically size each trade so that even a full loss only costs a small, controlled percentage of their total account.

5. Chasing losses

After a loss, it's natural to want to immediately win it back with a bigger, faster trade. This is called "revenge trading," and it tends to turn one manageable loss into a much bigger one, because decisions made from frustration are rarely good decisions.

Worth knowingNone of these mistakes are about intelligence. They're about emotional discipline under pressure — which is exactly why practising on a demo account, and starting small with real money, matters so much.

A better way to think about early losses

If you do lose money as a beginner, the useful question isn't "how do I win it back fast" — it's "what pattern led to this, and how do I avoid repeating it." Keeping a simple trade journal, even just a few lines per trade, is one of the most underrated habits in trading.
