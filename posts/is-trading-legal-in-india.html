Is Trading Legal in India? What Students Should Know

What SEBI and RBI actually allow, what's restricted, and why age matters more than most tutorials mention.

6 min read Money Moves India Last updated: 19 Aug 2026

This is one of the most searched questions by young traders in India, and the honest answer is: it depends on what you're trading, and where.

Stock trading (NSE/BSE)

Trading Indian stocks, index derivatives (like Nifty and Sensex options/futures), and commodities through a SEBI-registered broker is fully legal for Indian residents — provided you meet the age requirement. To open a trading and demat account, you generally need to be 18 or older, since these accounts require you to enter into a legal contract, and minors can't do that directly in most cases. Some brokers allow a minor's account operated by a parent or guardian, but the minor themselves cannot independently trade.

Forex trading — where it gets more complicated

This is the part most YouTube tutorials skip. Under India's Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), Indian residents are legally allowed to trade currency derivatives, but only through SEBI-registered exchanges and brokers, and only in pairs that involve the Indian rupee (like USD/INR, EUR/INR). Trading unrestricted global forex pairs (like EUR/USD) through unregulated overseas apps is a legal grey area at best, and the RBI has repeatedly warned that using unauthorised forex platforms can violate FEMA.

Worth knowingMany of the flashy "forex trading" apps promoted on social media by influencers are not SEBI-registered and are not legally authorised to serve Indian residents this way. Depositing money into them carries real legal and financial risk beyond normal trading risk.

The age question, specifically

If you're under 18, you cannot legally open a trading or demat account in your own name. This isn't a technicality that can be worked around with a workaround app — it's a basic contract law issue, since minors generally can't enter binding financial contracts in India. What some families do instead is open the account in a parent's name, with the parent fully aware and involved. If you're a student reading this: the right move is to learn everything you can now — through demo accounts and education — and open a real account once you're legally able to.

A simple checklist before using any platform

  • Is the broker SEBI-registered? (Check on the official SEBI website, not just the app's own claims)
  • If it's a forex platform, does it only offer INR-based pairs, in line with FEMA rules?
  • Are you old enough to legally hold the account in your own name?
  • Is a parent or guardian aware and involved, if you're using a family account?

None of this is meant to scare you away from learning — it's meant to make sure that when you do start trading with real money, you're doing it on solid legal ground.
