Understanding Currency Pairs: Majors, Minors & Exotics

Not all currency pairs behave the same way — here's how traders group them and why it matters.

6 min read Money Moves India Updated 2026

If you've spent any time looking at a trading app, you've probably noticed currency pairs get grouped into categories — majors, minors, and exotics. This isn't just labelling; it reflects real differences in how each pair behaves.

Major pairs

Major pairs always include the US dollar paired with another heavily traded currency, such as EUR/USD, USD/JPY, or GBP/USD. These are the most actively traded pairs in the world, which generally means tighter spreads and more predictable price movement.

Minor pairs

Minor pairs (sometimes called "crosses") involve two major currencies but exclude the US dollar — for example, EUR/GBP or AUD/JPY. They're still widely traded, just with somewhat lower liquidity than the majors.

Exotic pairs

Exotic pairs combine a major currency with the currency of a smaller or emerging economy — USD/INR falls into this category for most global platforms. Exotics tend to have wider spreads and can move more sharply on local news, since fewer participants are trading them at any given time.

Worth knowingWider spreads on exotic pairs mean a trade has to move further in your favour just to break even after costs — something beginners often don't account for when they start with a pair simply because it's familiar, like USD/INR.

Why this grouping matters

Liquidity affects how smoothly your orders get filled and how much it costs you to enter and exit a trade. Starting with major pairs while you're still learning gives you more predictable conditions to practise with, before moving to less liquid pairs later.
