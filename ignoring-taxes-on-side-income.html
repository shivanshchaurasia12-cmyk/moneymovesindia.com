Ignoring Taxes on Side Income Until It's Too Late

A mistake that has nothing to do with markets and everything to do with paperwork most beginners skip.

7 min read Money Moves India Updated 2026

It's a common assumption among students that side income — from trading, freelancing, or content creation — is too small or too informal to worry about at tax time. That assumption tends to cause real problems later.

Why this income doesn't fly under the radar

Income from trading, freelance work, or content creation is generally taxable in India once it crosses the applicable exemption threshold, regardless of whether it came from a "side hustle" rather than a formal job. Payments received via UPI, bank transfer, or platform payout all leave a trail that can be checked.

Why beginners tend to skip this

Tax rules feel complicated and distant when you're focused on actually earning the money in the first place — and many beginners simply aren't aware that trading profits and freelance income are treated differently by the tax system, each with their own rules.

What tends to catch people out

The most common issue isn't dishonesty — it's simply not keeping records as the income comes in, then facing a confusing pile of transactions to sort through months later, often after a deadline has already passed.

Worth knowingKeeping a simple running log of income and dates as you earn it — even a basic spreadsheet — makes an enormous difference at tax time compared to trying to reconstruct months of activity afterward. For anything beyond the basics, a chartered accountant is worth the cost.
