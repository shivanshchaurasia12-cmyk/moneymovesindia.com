What Is Affiliate Marketing? A Realistic Explainer

How commission-based promotion actually works, and why most affiliate income isn't passive at all.

7 min read Money Moves India Updated 2026

Affiliate marketing means earning a commission by recommending someone else's product or service through a unique tracked link — when someone buys through your link, you get a cut. It's genuinely accessible to start, but the realistic picture is more gradual than most "passive income" content around it suggests.

How the mechanics actually work

You sign up for an affiliate program (many major retailers and platforms run one), get a unique link tied to your account, and share that link wherever you have an audience — a blog, YouTube channel, Instagram, or WhatsApp group. When someone clicks your link and makes a purchase within a set window, the platform tracks it and you earn a percentage of that sale, or sometimes a flat fee.

Why an audience matters more than the link itself

The link is the easy part — anyone can sign up and get one in minutes. The actual bottleneck is having people who trust your recommendation enough to click it and buy. This is why affiliate marketing rarely works as a true first source of income; it works far better once you already have some audience, even a small one, built around a specific topic they came to you for.

Why relevance beats reach

A smaller, specific audience genuinely interested in a topic converts better than a larger, unrelated one — someone following you specifically for budgeting content is far more likely to click a link to a budgeting app than a random follower who found you through an unrelated trend. This is why niche content tends to outperform broad content for affiliate income, even with fewer total followers.

What "passive" actually means here

Affiliate income is often marketed as passive, but the honest version is: the work is front-loaded, not absent. Creating genuinely useful content that naturally recommends a product takes real effort upfront; the "passive" part only kicks in later, when that content keeps generating clicks and sales long after you published it, without additional daily work.

Being upfront about affiliate links

Disclosing that a link is an affiliate link — a simple, honest note — isn't just good practice, it's often a legal requirement depending on the platform and region, and it tends to build more trust with an audience than hiding it, not less. Audiences generally don't mind affiliate links on content that's genuinely useful; they mind feeling misled about why something was recommended.

Realistic numbers for a beginner

Commission rates vary widely by product category, often ranging from a small single-digit percentage on physical products to a larger share on digital products or subscriptions. With a small, engaged audience, early affiliate income is usually modest — often a few hundred rupees a month rather than anything life-changing — and it tends to grow slowly alongside your audience and content library, not in a sudden jump. Treating it as a long-term addition to other income sources, rather than a fast primary income, matches how it actually tends to play out.

Common early mistakes

A frequent one is dropping affiliate links into every single piece of content regardless of fit, which reads as spammy and erodes trust faster than it earns commission. Another is picking products purely based on which pays the highest commission rather than which your specific audience would actually want, leading to low click-through and even lower actual purchases. A third is expecting quick results and giving up within the first month, when affiliate income typically compounds slowly as both your content library and audience trust grow over time.

Worth knowingOnly recommend products you'd genuinely suggest even without earning a commission — an audience that senses you're pushing low-quality products purely for the payout stops trusting your recommendations fast.
