What Is Affiliate Marketing? A Realistic Explainer

How commission-based promotion actually works, and why most affiliate income isn't passive at all.

7 min read Money Moves India Updated 2026

Affiliate marketing gets pitched online as easy passive income — share a link, earn a commission. The mechanics are real, but the "passive" part is usually overstated, especially at the beginning.

How it actually works

A company gives you a unique tracking link to their product. When someone clicks your link and makes a purchase, you earn a percentage of that sale as a commission. Amazon Associates and various software or course affiliate programs work this way.

Where the traffic has to come from

The commission only happens if people actually click your link — which means you need an audience somewhere: a blog, a YouTube channel, an Instagram page, or similar. Building that audience is the real work, and it typically takes consistent effort over months, not days.

Why commissions are usually modest

Most affiliate commission rates for physical products are in the low single digits; digital products and software often pay more, sometimes 20–40%, because there's no manufacturing cost involved. Either way, it usually takes real sales volume to add up to meaningful income.

Worth knowingIn India, affiliate income counts as taxable income once you cross the applicable threshold, and Indian law also requires you to clearly disclose affiliate links to your audience — this isn't optional fine print, it's a real disclosure requirement.

A realistic way to start

Rather than chasing every affiliate program at once, most successful affiliates recommend picking one platform, one niche audience, and one or two relevant products, then being consistent for long enough to actually see whether it works for that specific audience.
